Research on a tilt-rotor UAV - a VTOL platform that hovers like a multirotor and cruises like a fixed-wing - focused on the transition regime where most of the hard control and propulsion problems live.

Overview

Tilt-rotor aircraft promise vertical takeoff with efficient cruise, but pay for it in mechanism complexity and a controls-heavy transition phase. The research scope covers propulsion sizing across hover and cruise, the tilt mechanism itself, and the control logic that has to hand off authority between rotor thrust and wing lift.

Problem

Transition flight is where tilt-rotor UAVs are hardest to certify and tune: thrust requirements drop as the wing starts producing lift, the propellers move through unsteady aerodynamic regimes, and the autopilot has to schedule gains across a quickly-changing plant. The challenge is making a transition profile that is smooth, repeatable, and safe to fly in simulation before any hardware risk is accepted.

Approach

  • Sized propulsion across hover, transition, and cruise design points; checked disk loading, thrust margin, and battery energy budget.
  • Built a CAD model of the tilt mechanism focused on stiffness, servo torque, and reliability under cyclic loading.
  • Modeled the airframe in Simulink with a quasi-steady aero model that captures lift transfer from rotors to wing during transition.
  • Prototyped transition control logic and stress-tested it in Gazebo / PX4 SITL before any hardware testing.

Contribution

  • Owned the propulsion sizing trade and the resulting design-point hover / cruise targets.
  • Built the CAD and analysis loop for the tilt mechanism, including a servo-torque margin check.
  • Set up the Simulink transition-flight model used to prototype the control schedule.

Outcome

  • A defensible propulsion sizing across hover, transition, and cruise that closes the energy budget for the target mission.
  • A simulation-validated transition profile that smoothly hands off between rotor and wing authority.
  • Bench-test plan and hardware risk list ready for the next phase of the program.

What I Learned

  • VTOL transition is a controls problem dressed as an aero problem - the schedule matters more than peak performance.
  • Mechanism design carries more risk than people expect; small geometry choices show up later as servo wear or vibration.
  • Sim-in-the-loop is the only honest way to evaluate transition logic before you commit to flight test.
