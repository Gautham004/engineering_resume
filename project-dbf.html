Owning the aero workflow for the OU AIAA Design/Build/Fly competition aircraft - from mission sizing through wind-tunnel validation and flight-test debrief.

Overview

DBF is a collegiate aerospace competition where teams design, build, and fly a fixed-wing aircraft against a yearly mission spec. As Aerodynamics Lead I drive the airfoil and planform choices that everything downstream - structures, propulsion, payload - has to live with.

Problem

The mission rewards a narrow band of cruise speed, payload fraction, and field length. The wing has to carry a heavy internal payload through tight turns without trading away the cruise drag we need to score laps. CFD alone won't tell us that - we need fast, comparable airfoil sweeps before any high-fidelity work is justified.

Approach

  • Screened candidate airfoils in XFOIL across the cruise and high-CL operating points; ranked on L/D, stall margin, and pitching moment.
  • Built an XFLR5 / AVL planform model to evaluate AR, taper, and tail volume against stability and trim drag.
  • Took the top candidates into ANSYS Fluent for 3D RANS checks at the cruise design point with a mesh-convergence study.
  • Coordinated wind-tunnel runs to validate CL/CD/CM and ground-truth the panel-method results.

Outcome

  • Converged on an airfoil + planform that hit the mission's payload band with margin on stall and trim.
  • Wind-tunnel data agreed with panel-method predictions inside the bands we cared about; flagged a Reynolds shift on stall.
  • Wrote the team's first aero design-review template - now used across structures and propulsion.
